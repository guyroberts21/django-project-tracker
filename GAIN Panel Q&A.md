### 2 - Panel Questions and Audience Q&A

#### Have you been involved in any other networks/groups beyond your day-to-day role at Barclays (ERGs, volunteering, GAIN etc.)?

**GTIS&CTO and BAU opportunities**
- GTIS&CTO Early Careers community - organising the previous (and first) Early Careers Townhall for our area. 
- I've helped out at a few one-off events specifically for early-careers. I often find there's so much to get out of these because people at the start of their careers are super eager to discover groups, and try new things out. 
- I think most business areas are keen on developing their early-careers communities. We're actually really good in GTIS&CTO, and there's a great team who puts on some amazing events. 
	- What I would suggest is that if your area doesn't have much early career presence currently, that you ask about it, and see if it is something you can either get more involved in, or support. 

**Mentoring:**
- I think everyone's always had a mentor. Even if that's not a formal agreement. We all have someone that we look up to, or someone we call when we're stuck or struggling, or someone who can really listen to you. For me, this has always been my parents. 

- But when I first started the grad scheme in 2023, I realised that it was great that I could chat to my parents about my work, but they didn't work at Barclays, they couldn't always fully understand things or suggest options etc. 

- So I looked for mentors at Barclays. Best decision I'd made. I started anxious and unconfident as a graduate, and I really really benefitted from first, being able to listen to someone else's story. 

- It was comforting to know I wasn't alone in feeling overwhelmed.

- Have had many mentors, and they've been the tipping point for me being bolder and more courageous in my decisions. 

- Very keen on mentorship. 
	- Barclays Mentoring App
	- Barclays Reverse Mentoring 
	- Career Coaching at Barclays 

And it's inspired me to mentor other people, and have confidence in doing it.

I've started mentoring a new joiner in our team in BCP - and I know how overwhelming it can be when you first join the team. So it feels really rewarding to be able to help someone in this position that I was once in. 

There are lots of opportunities etc - just try things out...
#### Why do you think it's important to get involved in activities beyond your role? And are there any particular lessons/benefits you'd like to share? 

- Network with new people 
- Develop new skills, either refine ones you are getting from your role currently OR things that you wouldn't get from this role.
	- Eg. For me, predominantly being in a developer role (where I can often be lost in the command line), it was really insightful to have an opportunity to try out a leadership role. 
- It also gives you a new platform to experiment. 
	- Whether that's in GAIN, or another ERG, or in your early careers community. 
	- Can take on more responsibility without as much pressure as an actual role in the bank might come with. 

- I think others will agree, that there are countless benefits to doing these kind of things. 
- When I think about opportunities and experiences, a good rule of thumb - at least for me - is that I want to be doing things that **make me go outside of my comfort zone**. 
	- Always leads to most growth/development. 

#### What advice do you have for grads/apprentices in terms of getting involved with things during their programme? 

- **Be proactive** - reach out to people. Ask how you can help. How you can get involved. 

- Think about **starting with the early-careers community** because there's so much opportunity to support, and you'll be helping people in similar positions to yourself. 

- **Make time for it.** When your normal BAU work becomes busy, it can be easy to think that other things like networking or developing other skills won't take priority. But its the collaboration of both your BAU development and the extra things you do that will lead to the most career development and growth, and unexpected opportunities.
